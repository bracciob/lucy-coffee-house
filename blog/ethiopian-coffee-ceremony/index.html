The Ethiopian Coffee Ceremony — Culture in Every Cup

How a thousand-year-old ritual still shapes the way we brew, pour, and gather.

In Ethiopia, coffee is never rushed. The ceremony — buna — can take hours. Green beans are washed, roasted over an open flame, and ground by hand. The room fills with the perfume of coffee blooming for the first time. It is at once a daily practice and a sacred one.

The brewed coffee is poured from a clay jebena into small handleless cups, always in three rounds: abol, tona, and baraka — the first for taste, the second for kindness, the third for blessing. To leave before the third round is considered impolite.

We built Lucy Coffee House to honor that patience. Our Yirgacheffe pour-overs take a few extra minutes because they should. Our weekend jebena service invites two guests to sit, share, and unhurry themselves for a while.

In a world that keeps asking us to move faster, the ceremony is a quiet act of resistance. It says: this cup matters, this person matters, this moment matters. That's the culture we brew.

Come taste the story in person.
